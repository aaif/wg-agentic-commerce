# [Report] Agentic Commerce – April 2026

**Working Group:** WG Agentic Commerce

**Reporting period:** April 2026

**Chair(s):** Ilya Grigorik (Shopify) & Rahul Bansal (OpenAI)

---

## Summary

The Working Group moved from broad landscape exploration into a draft charter and initial capability-map framing. April discussions mapped product discovery and checkout across ACP and UCP, while the charter workshop clarified the group’s protocol-neutral role, lifecycle scope, and focus on commerce-facing interfaces rather than underlying system internals.

## Progress Against Objectives

Work that would become the **Agentic Commerce Capability Map** advanced across discovery and checkout. The discovery analysis separated asynchronous product feeds from live merchant or marketplace retrieval and identified source discovery, normalization, freshness, and trust as distinct problems. The checkout analysis compared ACP and UCP as stateful, merchant-authoritative negotiations and surfaced extension, transport-binding, and human-handoff dimensions that a capability map must capture.

The charter draft also advanced substantially. The group defined a protocol-neutral remit across discovery, checkout, payment, and post-purchase; established the interface-versus-internals scoping rule; identified dependencies on identity, security, privacy, and observability work; and centered the initial deliverables on a shared capability frame, gap analysis, best practices, and a possible reference architecture.

## Blockers and Risks

Deliverable owners and timelines were not yet assigned. Representation also remained uneven across merchants and marketplaces, payment networks and issuers, and other affected ecosystem actors, creating a risk that the capability and gap analyses would reflect only part of the commerce stack.

## Decisions Needed from the TC

None. Charter review was already proceeding through its own review process.

## Next Month's Focus

1. Finalize the Working Group charter.
2. Compare payment-handler models in ACP and UCP, including instruments, credentials, delegation, and dynamic eligibility.
3. Examine Machine Payments Protocol (MPP) and whether its payment exchange can compose with broader checkout protocols.
