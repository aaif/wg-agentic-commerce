# [Report] Agentic Commerce – August 2026

**Working Group:** WG Agentic Commerce
**Reporting period:** August 2026
**Chair(s):** Ilya Grigorik (Shopify) & Rahul Bansal (OpenAI)

---

## Summary

The Working Group held two sessions that bracketed the transaction: a deep dive on the x402 payment protocol examined how value moves at the payment-delivery layer, and a PSP-led discussion of disputes and evidence examined how authorization, identity, and accountability must be reconstructed when a delegated purchase is later questioned. 

## Progress Against Objectives

The x402 session advanced the payment layer of the **Capability Map**. x402, now an independent foundation under the Linux Foundation, abstracts a single payment transaction rather than a cart checkout and composes as a payment rail within AP2, UCP, and ACP across HTTP, MCP, and A2A transports. Its decomposition into scheme, network, asset, and mechanism — with an optional facilitator bound to a narrow verify/settle contract — offered concrete structure for the payment-capability rows. Its deliberate boundary choices sharpened the **Gap Analysis**: intent and policy enforcement are left to the agent layer, discovery and reputation to ecosystem extensions, and refunds to extensions on rails where settlement is final. The presented split between conversational and autonomous commerce, including pay-per-use micropayments for API, content, and context access, widened the map beyond cart-shaped transactions.

The disputes session, building on June's fraud-signal discussion, examined how post-transaction accountability breaks when the cardholder was never present. Familiar evidence such as the click trail, device, and session behavior disappears in delegated flows, while the facts that now matter — delegation authority, intent scope and limits, agent identity and provenance — are not yet part of network burden-of-proof rules. Verifiable-intent records and agent identity (know-your-agent) emerged as the two candidate replacements for the absent human, alongside optional transaction metadata (platform, agent identifier, model version) whose value extends beyond fraud into reconciliation, monitoring, and buyer-facing receipts. The consent surface — merchant policies a present human implicitly accepts but an agent silently clicks through — was identified as a missing structured capability, and divergence across scheme-specific approaches was flagged as an adoption risk the group should help converge.
